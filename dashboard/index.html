Verdict: don't replace the bottom 10

Most of the "worst" machines are in a bad spot, not bad games. 9 of the 10 lowest earners sit in Back Forty, where the same game earns about 46% less per available hour than at the Main Entrance C2. Three of them were on the floor for under five months C1.

Luck explains almost everything else, with one exception. Across 310 machines the luck scores look like pure chance, except M-1158. It pays out on 38.6% of spins against a configured 31.5%, and held 3.0% against an expected 10.0% C4. That's a lead to inspect, not proof of wrongdoing.

Plan: $24,600 on three actions and keep $75,400. Inspect 2 machines, run one promotion weekend, and pilot a 2-pair floor remix (see actions).

Natural experiment: walkway closure C3

The Entrance–Back Forty walkway was closed for renovation from Mar 3 to Apr 7. Back Forty fell 28%, the rest of the floor rose 4%, and total floor theo per day did not fall. Play moved, it wasn't lost.

M-1158 (Desert Gold, Ridgeline, Back Forty) C4

Observed holdConfigured theo hold (10%)

The hit rate is the stronger evidence. Over about 674,000 spins, M-1158 paid something on 38.6% of spins. Its configured game (G-STD90) pays on 31.5%. That's a 124-standard-error gap, which luck can't produce. Every other machine is within ±3 standard errors of its configured hit rate. Possible causes: wrong paytable or game chip loaded, a meter or configuration-record error, or tampering. Inspect first, and make no accusations.

Downtime: real, but too small to move the decision

Faults per 100 machine-days, by manufacturer C5

Average wait for a technician, by shift C6

The graveyard shift runs one technician, and its waits average about 110 minutes. In April and May the day shift had about 0.9 technicians on duty against 1.7 planned (8 days with none in May), and May waits spiked. That's a scheduling fix that costs $0 from this budget.

Availability is 99.6%. All repair downtime over six months is about 4,900 machine-hours. At the floor's average of roughly $17 theo per available hour, that's about $14k a month at most, and most of that play moves to a neighboring machine (occupancy is about 36%). An extra technician costs $11,520. Cutting the graveyard wait by an hour saves roughly 50 machine-hours a month, about $1k. Not funded.

Demand and the forecast model

Floor coin-in is flat month to month and driven by the day of the week (Saturday is about 1.6× Tuesday) and by big weekend events. The model forecasts daily coin-in from the weekday average plus an event lift. It was trained on Jan–Apr and frozen, then tested on May–Jun.

How the model changes a decision

  • Promotion timing: the house's own slot tournament (Feb 21–23) beat its weekday forecast by +18.5% (≈ $82.5k extra theo), as much as the Super Bowl. The 7-day Locals Week added only +4.3%, so we fund a weekend format, not a week-long one.
  • Measuring it: the frozen weekday forecast is the counterfactual for the July 18–20 promotion. Lift = actual − forecast, compared with the model's ±$65k test MAE per day.
  • Staffing: forecast error is about 5% of a typical day. Weekend demand is predictable enough to schedule attendants and technicians by weekday rather than flat.

Floor remix: which machines to swap

The zone + game model treats demand as coming from the spot and spend rate from the game. Swapping a high-theo game out of Back Forty with a low-theo game at the Entrance or Pit raises expected floor theo without adding any players.

Tradeoffs: fully re-sorting the floor would add only about 3.6% theo per hour and would mean moving most of the floor, so remixing is a fine-tuning tool, not a rescue. The swap moves the 95%-RTP Royal Draw games away from the busiest spots, so guests there would see lower average payback. The pilot (top 2 pairs) tests the model before any wider change. Every swap avoids M-1158, which is under inspection.

Method, validation and evidence

Question→Fair comparison (theo, exposure, luck)→Competing explanations tested→Model on time holdout→Priced action + measurement

Model validation

No leakage: the training window ends Apr 30. Weekday averages and event lift are computed only from training days. Event types come from the events table, and every event was announced months ahead. No tourism or survey data is used as an input. Neither forecast was changed after looking at the test window.
