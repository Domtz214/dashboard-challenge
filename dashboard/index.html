Verdict: it's mostly location and luck, plus one leak

Most of the "worst" machines are in a bad spot, not bad games. 9 of the 10 lowest earners sit in Back Forty, where the same game earns about 46% less per available hour than at the Main Entrance C2. Three were on the floor for under five months C1.

Luck explains almost everything else, with one exception. 310 machines' luck scores look like pure chance, except M-1158. It pays out on 38.6% of spins against a configured 31.5%, and held 3.0% against an expected 10.0% C4. That's a lead to inspect, not proof of wrongdoing.

Location moves play rather than creating it. When the walkway into Back Forty closed, total floor theo didn't fall C3, so new machines or signs there wouldn't add much.

Over about 674,000 spins it paid something on 38.6% of spins, against 31.5% for its configured game. That's a 124-standard-error gap. Every other machine is within ±3. Possible causes: wrong paytable, a records error, or tampering. Inspect first, accuse no one.

Floor remix: which machines to swap

Demand comes from the spot and spend rate from the game. Swapping a high-theo game out of Back Forty with a low-theo game in a busy zone raises expected theo without adding players.

Tradeoffs: re-sorting the whole floor would add only 3.6%, so remixing is fine-tuning, not a rescue. Busy spots lose the 95%-RTP Royal Draw, so guests there get lower payback. Only the top 2 pairs are piloted, and M-1158 is never moved.

How the model changes a decision

  • Promotion format: the house's slot tournament weekend (Feb 21–23) added +18.5% (about $82.5k theo), as much as the Super Bowl. The week-long Locals Week added only +4.3%, so the plan funds a weekend.
  • Measuring it: the frozen forecast is the counterfactual for July 18–20. Lift = actual − forecast, judged against the ±$65k daily test error.
  • Staffing: forecast error is about 5% of a typical day, so attendants and techs can be scheduled by weekday instead of flat.
